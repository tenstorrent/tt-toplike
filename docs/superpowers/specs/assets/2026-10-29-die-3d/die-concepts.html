Looking into the chip while it thinks

Three ways to show the physical device lighting up and connecting. All three are animated. Tile positions and routes are illustrative, not read from a real card. Which one is closest to what you picture?

A. X-ray of the die

The die as it sits in silicon: GDDR6 on the two long edges, Ethernet on top, Tensix in the middle. Weights stream in from DRAM (left), cross the mesh as dots on the NoC wires, and each Tensix tile lights as it computes. Purple wires are NoC0, cyan wires are NoC1.

B. Physical and logical, side by side

The same activity drawn twice. Left is the die. Right is the NoC's own grid (the folded torus). One straight route on the right is a zig-zag on the left, so you can see why "1 hop" is about 2 cells of silicon and where the fold seam is.

C. Card cutaway

Zoomed out one level: the whole board. GDDR6 chips top and bottom, Ethernet ports left, PCIe and power right, the die in the middle. Data paths glow end to end: host over PCIe, into the die, out to GDDR. The die itself stays small and heat-coloured.

What each view needs from the hardware

  • A and B need per-tile traffic. Today we only have chip-wide numbers, so these animate from chip activity until we read per-tile NoC counters. That is the opt-in exalens mode from step 4.
  • C works with what we already read: PCIe bandwidth, GDDR activity, Ethernet link state, power. It is the only one that can be fully real in default mode.
  • The tile layout and the die-to-NoC coordinate fold are fixed per architecture, so the layout needs no device access.
