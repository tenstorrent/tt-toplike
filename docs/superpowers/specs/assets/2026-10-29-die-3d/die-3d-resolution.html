Less blocky: three terminal resolutions

The same scene drawn three ways. Each one is something a terminal can really print: coloured characters, nothing else. Compare the tile edges, the slopes of the faces and the GDDR blocks.

1. Braille: 2×4 dots per cell, 150 × 42 cells (300 × 168 dots)
2. Half-block (▀): 1×2 pixels per cell, 220 × 60 cells (220 × 120 pixels). Needs a 220-column terminal.
Before: half-block, 150 × 42 cells (150 × 84 pixels)

What each costs

  • Braille gives the finest picture at a normal width, about 4 times the pixels of the old view. Each cell holds only two colours, so a cell where three colours meet loses one. Edges look dotted, which suits a glowing wire but is soft on solid faces. It needs a font with braille glyphs (most terminal fonts have them).
  • Half-block at 220 columns keeps each pixel's own colour and looks clean, but needs a wide terminal. Narrower terminals would scale the scene down or fall back to the old size.
  • Both add a shading gradient on the faces and darker tile edges. That, more than resolution, removes most of the blocky look.
